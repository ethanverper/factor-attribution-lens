## Research — real institutional products actually studied

Browsed live via the in-app Browser tools and inspected with `getComputedStyle`, not reasoned from memory or screenshots. Honest access note up front: **actual institutional trading/risk terminals (Bloomberg Terminal, Goldman's internal Marquee platform, JPM's internal risk desk tools) are not publicly browsable** — nothing behind an institutional login is accessible from this environment. What follows is grounded in each firm's own public marketing/product sites, which is a real and legitimate register to study (it's how these firms present their own numbers and disclosures to the outside world) but is not the same as their internal desk tooling — flagged so this isn't oversold as "I looked at a real risk terminal."

- **goldmansachs.com** (homepage + `/insights` editorial index, live, `getComputedStyle`-inspected): headline type is a bespoke commissioned font family, `"GS Sans Condensed"` — not a Google Font, licensed the same way Mercury's "arcadia" was in decision 0012's original research. H1 weight is **400** (regular). The homepage's hero stats ("46K+", "1M+", "95%+") are set at **100px, weight 300 (light)** — dramatically large but deliberately thin, the opposite of "bold headline shouting a number." Every stat carries a small, gray, **sourced footnote directly beneath it** ("Sources: Headcount and external applicants as of 2024; 2023 Biennial Client & Stakeholder Survey…") — a real disclosure convention, not decoration. The palette is close to **monochrome**: scanning all rendered text/background colors on the homepage turned up only black/white/gray at varying opacities (`rgba(0,0,0,0.6/0.7/0.8/0.95)`) — no accent hue found anywhere on the page. CTAs are outlined (`border: 1px solid rgba(0,0,0,0.95)`, transparent background), not filled pills. The `/insights` index uses small all-caps category eyebrows ("MARKETS", "EXCHANGES", "TALKS AT GS") directly before each headline, followed by a date/format metadata line — structurally the same device as Factor Lens's own `[NN] SECTION` eyebrow, a second real validation of that pattern (the first was Linear's decimal-numbered nav in decision 0012).
- **jpmorgan.com** (homepage, live, `getComputedStyle`-inspected): another bespoke commissioned typeface, `"Amplitude"` / `"Amplitude Book"` — same lever as Goldman and Mercury, a third independent confirmation that real prestige financial/product brands invest in licensed type rather than a well-chosen Google Font. H2 weight is **300 (light)** at 40px. Body text color is `rgb(49,55,61)` — a dark warm-gray-navy, not pure black, consistent with the "near-black, not pure black" pattern from decision 0012's Linear research. The one accent color found (the "Read more" CTA) is a **muted, desaturated teal-blue**, `rgb(26,123,153)` — not a bright saturated brand blue — rendered as a small, solid-fill button, **14px, bold, uppercase, with 2.25px letter-spacing**: a distinctly institutional button convention (small and tracked, not a big rounded pill).
- **chase.com/personal/investments** (J.P. Morgan Wealth Management's retail-facing site, live): a genuinely useful contrast, not a repeat of the above. Chase uses **Open Sans** — a common, non-bespoke font — and a **bold, 700-weight H1** at the same 32px size Goldman/JPM run at 400. This is an honest, real finding: the bespoke-type/restrained-weight treatment is reserved for the *institutional/wealth* register (JPM.com, GS.com), not the *retail consumer banking* register (Chase's own investing page), which reads closer to conventional web design. The most concrete, reusable device found here: claims and offers carry **numbered inline footnote markers that open a disclosure overlay in place** — "Invest on your own… $0 commission online trades. `Footnote 1` `Opens overlay`" — a real, regulated-finance citation convention distinct from anything in the Stripe/Linear/Mercury research.

**The consistent, real pattern distinct from the startup-SaaS research**: institutional finance goes *further* into restraint than Stripe/Linear/Mercury did — headline weight drops to 300–400 (vs. the 480–510 found before), color is pulled toward near-monochrome (vs. "one accent, used sparingly" — GS's public site found effectively *zero* accent hue), and every claim/stat carries a citable disclosure, either inline (Goldman's under-stat footnote) or as an on-demand overlay (Chase's numbered footnote marker). Bespoke commissioned type is now a **three-for-three** finding (Mercury, Goldman, JPM) — it's not a Mercury idiosyncrasy, it's a real convention of brands that want to look expensive and serious.

## Decision — what changes, what's preserved

### 1. Preserved exactly, not touched

- The aperture/iris mark (`ApertureMark`) and its functional uses (masthead, favicon, frontier current-portfolio marker, loading state). Nothing in the institutional research argues against it — JPM/Goldman don't have an equivalent device, but "precise instrument" as a personality concept is not in tension with an institutional register; if anything it fits it better than it fit the startup-SaaS register.
- The graphite-navy dark mode, the amber `--primary`/`--chart-2` signal accent, and the **one-accent discipline** already audited in decisions 0013/0017 — Goldman's near-zero color and JPM's one muted teal both still land on "one functional accent or fewer," so this is validated, not contradicted. No new accent color is introduced.
- The `[NN]` bracket eyebrow nav/section-header system — doubly validated now (Linear's decimal system in 0012, Goldman's own Insights category-eyebrow convention here).
- JetBrains Mono for all data/numbers, and the load-bearing **weight-600 mono stat-tile values** (decision 0012's original carve-out). See §2 below for why this specifically does *not* get lightened despite Goldman's 300-weight hero numeral — the two numbers are doing different jobs.
- GSAP as the motion engine and decision 0015/0017's per-surface motion spec — nothing here calls for new animation infrastructure, only new places to point the existing mechanics (the Learning restructure in §3 reuses the exact reveal-timeline pattern already in `CiWhiskerDiagram.tsx`/`CapmDecompositionDiagram.tsx`, just re-gated).
- **Not adopting bespoke commissioned type.** This is a deliberate non-adoption, not an oversight: a licensed/commissioned typeface is a real lever (three-for-three in the research) but is a cost/scope commitment (licensing, or a genuine custom-type commission) that isn't justified for this project relative to what it buys — Space Grotesk at a restrained weight already reads as "not a default Google-Font vibe" per decision 0012's own original finding, and the type-weight change below closes most of the remaining gap. Flagged explicitly per the mandate to name tradeoffs rather than silently skip them.

### 2. What changes — the institutional register

**a. Headline weight, one targeted notch down — not a blanket change.** `SectionHeader`'s `h1` (`frontend/src/components/SectionHeader.tsx`, currently `font-medium` / 500, `text-[26px]`) drops to `font-normal` (400), matching Goldman's H1 (400) and sitting alongside JPM's H2 (300). This is the single biggest page-level element on every route and the most visible place to register the shift. **Do not** apply this to `CardTitle`/component-level headings (`[data-slot="card-title"]`, still 500 per decision 0012) — those are doing structural/navigational work at small sizes across dense grids (Tools & Technologies, References, Real World) and need to stay legible and distinct from body text, which a 400-weight card title at 15-16px would blur.

**b. Explicitly do NOT lighten the mono stat-tile values, despite Goldman's 300-weight/100px hero numeral.** This needs to be stated as a reconciliation, not silently ignored: Goldman's giant light-weight numeral is a *marketing* device (a headcount stat on a homepage, decorative-adjacent even with its footnote). Factor Lens's stat-tile values (CAPM β, R², frontier Sharpe, etc., in `StatTile`) are *read* numbers — a user is extracting an exact figure from them, the same job a trading blotter's mono figures do. That job calls for weight and tabular alignment, not lightness — decision 0012's original 600-weight mono carve-out for load-bearing stats was already right for the reason institutional data readouts (not institutional marketing pages) actually look the way they do. Preserve it.

**c. Stat-source citation convention, formalized and extended.** The Overview hero already does a version of this informally (`Overview.tsx` lines ~102-111: `CAPM β 1.00` next to `R² 50.3% · AAPL/MSFT/GOOGL/AMZN vs. S&P 500`, mono, muted). Formalize it as a named pattern — every headline stat callout (the Overview hero stat, and Results' primary stat tiles: CAPM beta, Fama-French R², frontier Sharpe) gets a one-line mono, `text-muted-foreground`, small-caps-style source citation directly beneath it (e.g. `Source: OpenBB (yfinance) · Kenneth French Data Library`), matching Goldman's under-stat footnote convention exactly. This is additive to Results (currently doesn't consistently carry this per-tile) and formalizes what Overview already does by instinct.

**d. A footnote-disclosure marker, Chase-style, replacing some inline caveat prose.** Several places in the app currently carry a methodology caveat as a full inline paragraph competing for reading attention alongside the main explanation — e.g. `LEARNING_CARDS`'s frontier card `note` field ("Long-only means the frontier never assumes…") rendered as a dashed-border paragraph in `Learning.tsx`, or the return/risk attribution card's annualization-convention aside buried in the middle of its `technical` paragraph. Add a small superscript numbered marker (`¹`, `²`…) inline at the exact claim it qualifies, using the **already-installed** `Popover` component (`frontend/src/components/ui/popover.tsx` — no new dependency) as the "opens overlay" surface, exactly mirroring Chase's "Footnote 1 — Opens overlay" pattern. This both adds the institutional citation device *and* declutters the always-visible prose, which directly supports rule 5's "not a wall of text" goal at the same time — two goals served by one change, not two separate additions. Apply this first to the Learning cards' `note` fields and the frontier/attribution technical asides; extending it to References & Formulas' own citations is a reasonable follow-on but not required here.

**e. Secondary-action buttons move toward JPM's small/bold/uppercase/tracked convention.** The Learning xref buttons ("See the exact regression →") and similar tertiary in-content links currently render as `variant="outline" size="sm" rounded-full` pill buttons. Restyle this specific tier (not the primary `Run analysis`/`Enter your holdings` CTAs, which should stay obviously-clickable filled buttons — usability, not register, wins there) to a small, non-pill, uppercase, letter-spaced treatment: `text-[10.5px] font-bold uppercase tracking-[0.12em] border-b border-current rounded-none px-0` or equivalent — text-styled with an underline rather than a boxed pill, closer to JPM's small tracked CTA than a generic SaaS pill button.

**Personality line, refined** (was: *"looking through a precise instrument — quiet, exact, nothing decorative"* — decision 0012): **Factor Lens should read like the analytical desk of a real institution — an internal risk tool a bank would actually use, not a startup dashboard: near-monochrome by default, exactly one functional accent, and every claim traceable to a citation.** The aperture-mark "precise instrument" idea is preserved and folded in, not replaced — this is a sharpening of the same personality, grounded in a more specific register.

### 3. Learning restructure — progressive, interactive, Udemy-style

Concrete component-level spec against `frontend/src/pages/Learning.tsx` / `frontend/src/data/learning.ts`, using shadcn primitives already available in this project (`components.json` confirms `style: radix-vega`; `Accordion`, `Popover`, `Collapsible` are already installed and in use elsewhere — only `Progress` is new, `npx shadcn add progress`).

**a. Curriculum framing + progressive disclosure.** Replace the flat `LEARNING_CARDS.map` → always-expanded `Card` stack with an `Accordion` (`type="single" collapsible`, defaulting to the first item open) — the exact same primitive already used for Glossary's grouped terms (`Glossary.tsx`), but here it's a **sequenced curriculum**, not a flat reference list, so it should behave differently: only one concept expanded at a time (not `type="multiple"` like Glossary), reinforcing "work through this in order" the way a course does. Each `AccordionTrigger` shows a numbered badge (`01`–`04`, mono, matching the existing `[NN]` convention) plus the title; the plain-language paragraph's *first sentence only* stays visible in the collapsed header as a teaser (Udemy's own lesson-list pattern — you see what a lesson covers before opening it), with the rest of the plain-language paragraph, the technical register, the diagram, and the xrefs inside `AccordionContent`.

**b. Real progress tracking.** Add a `Progress` bar (new shadcn component) directly under `SectionHeader`, bound to a `viewedIds: Set<string>` tracked in component state via the `Accordion`'s `onValueChange` (Radix hands back the newly-opened item's value) — persist this set to `sessionStorage` under a fixed key (e.g. `fl-learning-viewed`) so it survives navigating away and back within a session without inventing backend state. Render `"{viewedIds.size} of 4 concepts explored"` as a small mono label next to the bar, and give each `AccordionTrigger` a small check icon (`lucide-react`'s `Check`, `text-success`) once its id is in the set, next to the numbered badge. Non-gating — every concept stays clickable regardless of order, consistent with how real course UIs let you skip around; only the numbering/progress communicates sequence, nothing blocks it.

**c. Required interactive check — CI whisker predict-then-reveal.** `CiWhiskerDiagram.tsx` currently reveals its two example rows (point estimate → whisker → verdict) purely on scroll-into-view, with both verdicts ("✓ real exposure" / "≈ inconclusive") always shown once revealed. Convert it into a genuine check: show only the point estimate and whisker for both rows on reveal, then two clickable options above the diagram ("Example A is the real exposure" / "Example B is the real exposure"), and hold the verdict text/color hidden until the reader picks one. On click, reveal that row's verdict (reusing the exact existing GSAP opacity-stagger mechanism, just re-gated from `useInView` to the click handler) plus one line of feedback ("Correct — Example A's interval never crosses zero." / "Look again — Example B's interval crosses zero, which is why it's inconclusive even though the point estimate itself is nonzero."). This is the single most load-bearing analytical skill in the whole app (reading a CI whisker is exactly what a user needs to do on the real Results charts moments later), so testing it here is a real check, not interactivity for its own sake.

**d. Recommended (not required) interactive check — a manipulable CAPM diagram.** `CapmDecompositionDiagram.tsx` currently draws three fixed-width blocks (`SEG1`/`SEG2`/`SEG3` are hardcoded pixel widths). As a stretch item if time allows: add a small illustrative `Slider` (reusing the exact `Slider` component already built for Inputs' allocation controls) letting the reader drag an illustrative β from 0.4–1.6 above the diagram, with the "market exposure" block's width animating live via `gsap.to()` on the rect's `width` attribute as the slider moves — "Try it: drag β" labeled, with the same "illustrative, not your data" disclosure already in the diagram's caption. This is real, hands-on manipulation of a claim (rule 5's "an example the reader can manipulate"), not just a second predict-then-reveal — flagged as recommended rather than required so scope stays clear if `developer` needs to triage.

**e. Frontier diagram — leave the reveal mechanism as-is, do not force a third interactive check onto it.** `FrontierPositionDiagram.tsx`'s existing scroll-triggered arrow reveal (two labeled arrows for the "more return, same risk" / "same return, less risk" dual reading) is already the diagram's own "aha" moment — bolting an unrelated click-interaction onto it to hit a quota would be interactivity for its own sake, which rule 11 explicitly warns against. Two real interactive checks (c) plus one manipulable diagram (d) is the right count for a four-concept page, not four out of four.

**f. Return/risk attribution card stays text+identity-only, as today** — it's an exact algebraic identity already shown as real notation and cross-linked to a live table; nothing here calls for a fourth diagram or a third interactive check, consistent with `educator`'s original Phase 9e reasoning for leaving it text-only.

## Consequences / flags for `developer` and `pm`

- **One new shadcn component**: `Progress` (`npx shadcn add progress`). Everything else in this spec (`Accordion`, `Popover`, `Collapsible`, `Slider`, `Badge`) is already installed and in use elsewhere in the app — confirmed by reading the actual `frontend/src/components/ui/` files before writing this spec, not assumed.
- **Real, scoped implementation surface**: `frontend/src/index.css` (one selector weight change), `frontend/src/components/SectionHeader.tsx` (h1 weight), `frontend/src/components/AppSidebar.tsx` (credit line), `frontend/src/pages/Overview.tsx` (colophon line), `frontend/src/pages/Learning.tsx` + `frontend/src/data/learning.ts` (curriculum Accordion + progress state), `frontend/src/components/diagrams/CiWhiskerDiagram.tsx` (predict-then-reveal gating), `frontend/src/components/diagrams/CapmDecompositionDiagram.tsx` (optional slider). No route/architecture change, no new client dependency beyond one shadcn primitive.
- **Explicit reconciliation, not silent override**: §2b above is the one place this spec's own research (Goldman's light-weight hero numeral) argues for a change that is then deliberately *not* made, with the reason stated — flagged so `developer` doesn't "helpfully" apply it to stat tiles on their own reading of this doc.
- **Bespoke type was considered and explicitly declined** (§1) — worth revisiting only if a future round of feedback specifically asks for it, given the licensing/commissioning cost that implies.
- **Nothing here touches** `app/models/`, `app/data/`, `POST /api/analysis`, or any other validated backend logic — this is presentation-layer only, same boundary every prior identity/motion decision in this project has respected.
